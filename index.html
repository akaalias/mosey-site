The Nudge

Twenty minutes at the desk, then a walk. No pressure, ever.

You don't schedule a walk. You arm a rhythm. Every twenty minutes the belt starts and holds the door at a crawl; step on when you're ready, and the moment the pad feels your steps the walk takes its own pace and its own hills. Three minutes or twenty, then it eases down, stands by for twenty, and picks the next one.

The day opens short — the mailbox, the corner shop — and every walk you join makes the next ones a little longer. Skip one and nothing is lost. Step off mid-walk and nothing is wrong; the rest just started early. Nothing counts down at you, nothing turns red, nothing keeps a streak.

The Round Book

Fifty-two walks with a reason.

Not workouts. The mailbox at three minutes. The coffee run up the hill at five. The lock on the canal at lunch. The trailhead twenty minutes from the office. Ten places and Anywhere, three to twenty minutes each — the length is the first thing you see.

Every walk choreographs pace and climb — the pad tilts where the story climbs. The ring draws it: radius is your pace, thickness is the hill, light is how far you've come. Two dots walk it: the guide, and you.

Which pads

Any pad that speaks Bluetooth FTMS.

Mosey has no pad-specific code: it looks for the standard Fitness Machine Service and drives whatever answers. Verified on the Sportstech sPad500; documented on KingSmith's Z1, R3 and X218, Urevo's SpaceWalk 3S, Mobvoi, WELLFIT and every pad that ships with the FitShow app. KingSmith's older models, DeerRun and Sperax run their own protocols and won't.

Should workTwenty-odd pads with FTMS documented by Zwift, Kinomap or the QZ project. Without a step count the walk starts at once, as it did before.
