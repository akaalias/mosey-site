Mosey 1.2 went to App Review on the third of September and came back approved a week later. In between I kept walking, and the version on my own Mac drifted a good way from the one Apple was reading. This is the list, in the order it happened, because every one of these came from a walk and not from a plan.

## The rhythm was the wrong shape

The Blue Zones finding says *get up every twenty minutes and move*. It never says for how long. The first book had every walk at twenty-five minutes and the rest at twenty-five too, which is a workout with a break in it. Now the rest is the constant, twenty minutes, and the walks have their own lengths: the mailbox is three minutes, the trailhead is twenty, and fifty other reasons to be out sit in between. The minutes are the first thing you see on every screen, and the index lists the walks by how much time you have.

## The belt holds the door

A walk used to start at two kilometres an hour whether or not anyone was on the belt. Getting onto a moving belt at two is a small threshold; at one it is barely one, and one is markedly quieter. So the belt now opens at a crawl and waits. The moment the pad feels your steps, the walk takes its own pace and its own hills. Step off, and it eases back to the crawl within a few seconds.

Feeling your steps turned out to be possible. I had written off the pad as blind to the walker in August, because listening to its Bluetooth said nothing. It said nothing because nobody had asked: the pad's own console counts footfalls from the motor, and the count is there for the asking, one query a second. Zero on an empty belt, sixty-odd a minute with me on it. That count is now the second dot on the ring.

## Two on the ring

A walk is drawn as one line, and there used to be one dot on it: where you are. Now there are two. The white one is the guide, walking the round at its pace from the moment it begins. The coloured one is you, waiting at the start until the pad feels you, then catching the guide up and walking with it. When you catch up, a singing bowl sounds, three soft strikes, a little different every time. It is the nicest thing in the app and it cost a Tuesday.

## The Nudge listens

The short walks at the start of the day were the best part, and then the rhythm felt robotic: it never noticed whether I'd walked the last round. Now it does. The day opens on the short walks, and every walk you actually join lifts the ones after it a rung. Skip one and nothing is lost; the rung holds. By the afternoon, if you've walked every round, you're on the twenty-minute ones. Nothing shows this. It should just feel natural.

## Smaller things

The index is a contents page now, like a book's, instead of a grid of small rings. The buttons are glass, lit from above. The Nudge's button says *Begin the day*, because that is what it does. And the arrow keys turn the Round Book's pages.

## What this means for the store

1.2 is out as reviewed, with the twenty-five-minute rhythm. 1.3, with everything above, is submitted, and the store page already describes it. If you're reading this between the two, the app you download will catch up in a few days.
