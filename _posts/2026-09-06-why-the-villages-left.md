---
title: Why the villages left
place: Berlin
description: Mosey's walks used to be set in the Blue Zones themselves — four villages, seen through the eyes of their oldest residents. Here's why that was wrong, and what survived.
---
For most of August, every walk in Mosey took place in a Blue Zone. There was a Sardinian village with a passeggiata at dusk. There was an Okinawan coast road in the morning, a garden lane where someone's grandmother looked up from her vegetables. Fifteen walks, then twenty, across two invented towns with real DNA, and each one framed from inside a long life: the ninety-year-old who has watered those balcony plants every evening for forty years.

I loved it. I also stopped using it in about a week, which is the only review that counts.

## What was wrong

On the first of September I wrote the note that killed it: *stop using the point of view of a hundred-year-old as the basis for framing when I say Blue Zone.* Too centenarian. Too four-villages. Too cute.

I'm forty. I work at a desk in Berlin. The walks I actually take are: down to the river when the laptop finally shuts. The long way from the station because the shortcut takes four minutes and that is exactly the problem with it. A phone call that's too good for the couch. The school drop-off. Beating the rain. None of these happen in a village with a bakery. A book of walks set in someone else's very long life is a postcard, and you don't walk through postcards twice.

## What survived

The core idea did, completely: a walk needs a reason, and *exercise* is not one. That's the Blue Zones' whole point about movement, and it's the one thing the first version got right. The shepherd isn't exercising. Neither is the woman with the plants.

So the new book keeps the reason and changes the person. Fifty walks now, five each for ten places, New York to Tokyo to Norway to Anywhere, written for a working adult with a working adult's excuses. The pad still starts itself every twenty-five minutes, which is the Blue Zones' every-twenty-minutes with a rest built in. The villages are gone. The village's idea about walking is the entire app.

If you have a walk that belongs in the book, the one you actually take, I want to hear it. It's probably better than the one I'd invent.
